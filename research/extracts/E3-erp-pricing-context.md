## Main findings (relevant subset)

1. **Market base.**
   - BANBEIS: **36,710+** post-primary institutions, **20.2M+** students. (The GTM doc says 36,940 and 18.2M.)
   - Private MPO secondary: **19,757** (93.7% of secondary). Government secondary: **691** (3.3%).
   - **3,341** colleges and **1,500** school-and-colleges.
   - Madrasahs **9,259–9,291**.
   - English-medium: **137 formal + ~1,000 unlisted**, with tuition of **BDT 6,000–30,000+/month**.
   - Government secondary fees are nominal: **BDT 15–50/month**.
2. **Operational overhead is self-funded.** MPO schools receive teacher salary subsidies, but all operating overhead ("examination processing, and administrative software") comes from internal collections.
3. **Regulated fee caps constrain budgets.** Per government gazette, session-charge caps for MPO institutions are:
   - **BDT 5,000** in Dhaka metro
   - **BDT 3,000** in divisional cities
   - **BDT 2,000** in district municipalities
   - **BDT 1,500** in upazilas
   - Non-MPO: **BDT 7,500–8,500** in Dhaka metro; **BDT 3,000–6,000** in districts and upazilas
   - **[Analyst note]** This undercuts E1's idea of passing a new "Digital Assessment Fee" to parents at MPO schools.
4. **WTP by segment for a full ERP (BDT/yr):**

   | Segment | Students | WTP (BDT/yr) | Procurement |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | SEG-1 rural/small MPO | 200–500 | **15k–25k** | "Conservative SMC; highly price-averse" |
   | SEG-2 urban/district MPO | 500–1,200 | **35k–65k** | SMC requires competitive quotes |
   | SEG-3 urban private non-MPO | 400–1,500 | **60k–120k** | Founder/principal-driven, fast |
   | SEG-4 high-volume colleges | 1,500–4,500+ | **100k–250k** | Governing Body + finance sub-committee |
   | SEG-5 elite EM & trust chains | 500–2,500 | **250k–750k+** | Corporate board/trustees |
5. **Current spending lines (BDT/yr):**
   - Clerical staff (loaded): **180k–480k per person**; National Pay Scale grades 16–20 basic **BDT 8,250–20,010**
   - Printing, ledgers and receipts: **45k–180k**, for 3 exam cycles/yr
   - Bulk SMS: **25k–120k** at **BDT 0.40–0.65/SMS** and **4–8 SMS/student/month**
   - Software maintenance: **10k–45k**
   - IT upkeep: **20k–60k**
   - Bank reconciliation: **15k–50k**
6. **Actual software spend today** is very low: "typically **BDT 0 to 15,000/year**" on pirated tools or static websites. Local "scripts" sell for **BDT 2,000–10,000** (elsewhere **2,250–15,000**) one-off. Past purchases of about **BDT 30,000** one-off are common objections.
7. **Labour rates:**
   - Office staff loaded **BDT 100–130/h** (BDT 15–20k/month over 154 h)
   - Senior teachers and headmasters doing admin **BDT 200–250/h**; the model uses **BDT 180/h** for teachers
8. **Exam tabulation is the costliest admin workflow.** In the 800-student example:
   - **24 teachers × 35 h per exam cycle × 3 cycles = 2,520 h = BDT 453,600/yr**
   - Attendance: 1,100 h, BDT 198,000
   - Fee handling: 1,080 h, BDT 129,600
   - Printing: BDT 85,000
   - Total status-quo cost: **BDT 866,200**
9. **Result errors:**
   - **2–5%** of published score sheets need manual correction, costing **BDT 20,000–50,000 per exam cycle**
   - Board registration mismatches: **15–40/yr** at **BDT 1,500–3,500** each
10. **Savings taxonomy.** Hard savings (cash), soft savings (hours redeployed), avoided costs. The doc explicitly warns: **do not sell on staff reduction**. Clerks' jobs are politically protected, and the pitch triggers resistance from the people evaluating the system.
11. **Competitor ERP prices (the most verifiable data):**

    | Vendor | Pricing |
    |---|---|
    | Smart Software | **BDT 10/student/month** (100–250 students) down to **BDT 5** (801–2,000); BDT 10,000 setup |
    | MSMHS | **BDT 12,000/yr** (≤200 students) to **BDT 49,999/yr** (1,000+) |
    | 71WebHost/My-SoftIT | **BDT 50,000 setup + BDT 5/student/month** to **BDT 200,000 setup + BDT 12/student/month** |
    | EduGradUP | **BDT 8/student/month** |
    | Shebashikkha | **BDT 10–20/student/month** |
    | BD School Pro | **BDT 6,000–10,000/yr** |
    | Shahed Tech | **BDT 2,000/month or 15,000/yr**, up to **80,000/yr** or **200,000 lifetime** |
    | Fedena | ~**BDT 12–18+/student/month**, plus **$200–500** implementation |

    Summary: mid-market SaaS runs at **BDT 5–10/student/month**; enterprise at **BDT 150k–500k+/yr**.
12. **Van Westendorp estimate for a 1,000-student mainstream school (full ERP):**
    - Too cheap: **<BDT 15,000/yr**
    - Bargain: **BDT 25–35k**, which the **principal can approve without committee**
    - Expensive but viable: **BDT 45–65k**, which needs an SMC meeting
    - Too expensive: **>BDT 100,000/yr**, viable only for elite schools
13. **Minimum viable ACV.** Below **BDT 25,000/yr per institution** the business is "economically unattractive": it cannot fund support or training, and churn rises. Strongest perceived value is at **BDT 45–65k/yr**.
14. **Value capture.** Target **10–12%** of economic value created. The balance-sheet table shows actual capture of **5.3–8.0%**. Recommended prices: rural BDT 20k, urban MPO 45–48k, non-MPO 75k, college 160–170k, elite EM **BDT 350k**.
15. **Price elasticity:**

    | Price (BDT/student/month) | ~ACV (BDT) | Close rate | Churn | GM |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | 2.00 | 20k | 26–30% | 20–25% | 55–60% |
    | 4.50 | 54k | 16–20% | 6–8% | 78–82% |
    | 9.00 | 108k | 5–7% | 4–6% | — (elite-only) |
16. **Payment methods (fee collection by schools):**
    - About **65%** of mid-sized institutions collect cash at the counter
    - **25%** use bank-branch deposit challans
    - **10%** use informal bKash/Nagad to a staff wallet
    - Bad debt runs **4–8%** of billable tuition
    - MFS merchant discount rate: **1.50–1.85%**; convenience fees **BDT 10–15**/transaction
17. **How institutions pay vendors.** Through the **head clerk/cashier via joint bank cheques** drawn on institutional accounts.
18. **Six-stage buying process:**
    1. End users (teachers, clerks)
    2. Influencer (assistant head / senior ICT teacher; checks Bangla support and NCTB fit)
    3. Champion (headmaster/principal)
    4. Evaluator (SMC finance/procurement sub-committee, which compares against **print/paper/stationery budgets**)
    5. Approver (SMC chairman / trustees)
    6. Payer (head clerk/cashier)
    - Sales should equip the headmaster with a **one-page financial comparison**.
19. **Budget and sales calendar:**
    - **Nov–Jan:** admissions and session-fee collection; **peak cash and peak buying window**
    - **Feb–Apr:** prospecting and demos
    - **May–Jun:** half-yearly exams; **"high pain window" for an exam pilot**
    - **Jul–Aug:** follow-ups; proposals to the SMC
    - **Sep–Oct:** pre-test/model exams; finalise approvals for next year
20. **Contract structure:**
    - **Monthly billing is "commercially unviable"**
    - Term/bi-annual billing allowed by exception with a **5% surcharge**
    - **Annual upfront is standard: invoice Dec 1, due Jan 15**
    - 2–3-year prepay at **15–20%** off
    - Price indexation **5–7% every two years**, with **90 days' notice** before the December renewal
    - For cash-constrained schools: **20% deposit, 80% after January admissions**
21. **Pilot design.** A **paid** "Single-Terminal Exam Evaluation Pilot":
    - 1 grade (~150 students), 1 terminal exam
    - **BDT 5,000 non-refundable**, 100% credited to the annual contract
    - Unrestricted free trials and freemium are "commercially ineffective" and should be avoided
    - First offer to test: **50 paid pilots at BDT 5,000** in the half-yearly cycle
22. **Implementation must be paid.** BDT 8,000–25,000 (enterprise BDT 50–150k). Free onboarding leads to stalled adoption. Adoption rebate: **BDT 5,000** if 95% of data is complete within 60 days.
23. **CAC / churn:**
    - Field sales: **BDT 18,000** (18–22% close)
    - Resellers: **BDT 11,000** (15% of Year-1 contract value)
    - Inbound/word of mouth: **BDT 6,500** (35–45% close)
    - Blended: **BDT 14,500**
    - Base churn **7.5%**; LTV **BDT 586,656**; LTV/CAC **40×**; payback **3.95 months**
    - Stress case (15% churn, CAC 25k): **11.7×**, **6.8 months**
24. **Discounts.** Network of 3+ campuses: 10%. Reference-site credit: BDT 3,000. Per-student and usage rates are non-discountable.

## Buyer / decision maker / user / payer analysis

- **Users:** class teachers and accounts clerks.
- **Influencer:** assistant head / senior ICT teacher. Checks **Bangla UI support and NCTB compliance**.
- **Champion:** headmaster/principal.
- **Evaluator:** SMC finance sub-committee, which benchmarks against **print/paper/stationery budgets**.
- **Approver:** SMC chairman / Governing Body / trustees. For elite EM: board, MD or owner.
- **Payer:** head clerk/cashier, by **joint-signature bank cheque**.
- **Government schools:** procure via **DSHE e-tenders**. **[Analyst note]** Effectively out of reach for an early-stage product.
- **Fast deciders:** SEG-3 urban private non-MPO. **Slow, price-averse:** SEG-1 rural MPO.

## Pricing model(s) proposed (for ERP, context only)

The ERP doc recommends a hybrid of platform fee and bracketed per-student tiers:

| Tier | Students | Price | Setup |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shiksha Start | <400 | BDT 20k/yr flat | BDT 8k |
| Prothoma Core | 401–1,000 | BDT 15k + BDT 3.50/student/month (ACV 40–57k) | BDT 15k |
| Uttoron Pro | 1,001–2,500 | BDT 25k + BDT 5/student/month (ACV 85–175k) | BDT 25k |
| Enterprise | 2,500+ | BDT 250k–750k+ | — |

Other elements: metered SMS at wholesale + 25–30%; payment-gateway rev-share of BDT 1.50–3.00/transaction; "per-user" pricing is prohibited; enrollment bands are reconciled once a year in January.

## Unit economics & cost assumptions (context only)

- ERP serving COGS: **BDT 12,000/institution/yr** (compute 5,400; backups 1,800; gateway/SSL 1,200; L1 support 3,600)
- ARPU **BDT 56,000**; GM **78.6%**; blended ARPU in simulation **BDT 54,200**
- Onboarding **BDT 16,500** average
- Simulated scale: 1,000 institutions ≈ **BDT 66.2M** revenue

These are ERP figures and are **not** transferable to AI grading, which has meaningful per-unit inference and review cost.

## GTM implications (sales process, pilot, onboarding, channels)

- Sell in **Nov–Jan** (cash).
- Pilot in **May–Jun**, the half-yearly exam window; this is also the pain window for exam grading.
- Propose to the SMC in **Jul–Aug**; lock approvals in **Sep–Oct**.
- Use **paid, credited pilots** (BDT 5,000) rather than free trials.
- Charge for onboarding; give an adoption rebate.
- Channels: field reps (CAC ~18k), resellers (15% of Year-1), inbound/referrals. Reference-site incentives, because headmasters trust neighbouring headmasters.
- The **exam tabulation / report card** workflow is named as the single most labour-intensive and the best pilot wedge. **[Analyst note]** This is directly adjacent to AI script grading, so a combined "grade + tabulate + report card" pilot is the natural offer.

## Assumptions

1. Institutional WTP is bounded by visible budget lines: print, SMS, clerical overtime.
2. The SMC approves against a one-page savings comparison.
3. Annual billing aligned with January session fees is mandatory.
4. Cheaper-than-BDT-15k software is perceived as unreliable or pirated.
5. Parents will pay small MFS convenience fees.
6. Churn is 7.5%.

## Recommendations (relevant)

- Price against the cost of the problem, but capture only **~5–12%** of value.
- Avoid freemium and unmetered usage for any variable-cost item (the SMS lesson applies directly to AI inference).
- Bill annually in advance.
- Use paid, credited pilots.
- Never pitch staff reduction.

## Critical assessment

- The evidence-tag framework is good practice, but tags are over-applied. "OBSERVED" claims have no audit trail. The Van Westendorp section uses the method's vocabulary without any survey.
- Figures conflict with the GTM doc: institutions 36,710 vs 36,940; students 20.2M vs 18.2M; EM 137 formal vs 123–168 vs E1's 350+.
- The revenue simulation reports a constant 81.8% GM at every scale and double-uses auxiliary revenue equal to COGS. It is illustrative only.
- **Most useful and credible parts:** published competitor price points; MPO fee caps; the procurement roles; the Nov–Jan cash window; the annual-billing norm; the anti-freemium and anti-unmetered lessons.
- There is still no primary WTP research. **[Analyst note]** All three docs share this gap.

## Confidence + Relevance to product decisions

- **Confidence: Medium** for market structure (vendor prices, fee caps, procurement roles, calendar). **Low** for WTP bands and CAC/churn.
- **Relevance: High as a constraint.** The key facts:
  - Mainstream Bangladeshi schools pay about **BDT 42–60/student/yr** for an entire ERP.
  - Principals may self-approve roughly **≤BDT 25–35k/yr**.
  - Payment is by annual cheque in Nov–Jan.

  These cap what an AI grading product can charge outside premium and coaching segments. They argue for: an ERP-bundle/API distribution path; very low per-script COGS for the mass market; hard usage metering (no unlimited plans); and a paid, credited exam-cycle pilot aligned with the May–Jun half-yearly exams.
