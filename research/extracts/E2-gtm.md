## Main findings

1. **Positioning.** Sell an **"Assessment Intelligence Platform"** with Human-in-the-Loop (HITL). Do not sell an "AI Exam Checker", which is framed as a threat with high teacher resistance, or an "AI Grading Assistant", which reads as a utility with low WTP.
2. **Beachhead** (stated slightly differently in three places):
   - Exec summary: Tier-1 and Tier-2 private English-medium schools, English-version institutions, and large coaching chains in **Dhaka and Chattogram**.
   - §6: **Private English-Version and top private Bangla-Medium schools in Dhaka, plus commercial admission coaching chains**.
   - First-5 customers: "tech-forward English Medium & Elite Bangla Schools".
   - Rationale given:
     - Over **70%** of premium private schools and coaching HQs are in Dhaka clusters (Dhanmondi, Gulshan, Banani, Uttara, Mirpur)
     - Coaching weekly mock tests make grading delays hurt retention
     - English scripts are easier for OCR than Bangla cursive
     - There is no public procurement
3. **Motion.** Founder-led field sales first (customers 1–15), then outbound field reps (16–100), with ERP/RMS vendor partnerships as the scale channel. Pure PLG is "not recommended for B2B", though a teacher-champion free tier is still proposed.
4. **Commercial model.** Hybrid: annual base fee + **BDT 8–15 (≈$0.07–0.13) per evaluated script**.
5. **Unit-economics targets.** CAC payback **<3 months**, LTV/CAC **>15×**. The KPI box targets GM **≥80%** and NRR **≥115%**.
6. **Market context.** "Over 18 million post-primary students" generate "hundreds of millions" of handwritten scripts a year.
7. **TAM:**

## Assumptions

1. English-version scripts are materially easier to machine-read than Bangla, so the beachhead can avoid the hardest OCR problem early.
2. Coaching chains grade large volumes of *written* scripts. **[Analyst note]** Admission-prep testing in Bangladesh is heavily MCQ/OMR, which already has cheap solutions; this needs validating.
3. Institutions have or will accept feed scanners.
4. Institutions will pay about BDT 10/script, or ~BDT 1M/yr for a 2,000-student school.
5. Teacher time savings convert to willingness to pay.
6. ERP vendors want to resell and will integrate quickly (first integration live within 60 days).
7. A 70% pilot-to-paid rate.
8. 4-year retention.
9. ~88% character-level Bangla ICR is "good enough" with HITL.
10. The Bangla engine can be reused in West Bengal/Assam.
11. The Oct–Jan budget window is when deals close.

## Recommendations (as made by the doc)

1. Position as Assessment Intelligence with mandatory teacher sign-off.
2. Focus on Dhaka private EV/elite Bangla schools and coaching chains.
3. Use founder-led sales for customers 1–15, then field reps; build ERP partnerships in parallel.
4. Run strict 14-day, capped, commitment-backed pilots with ≥90% agreement and >60% time-reduction gates.
5. Price BDT 8–15/script plus an annual platform fee, with a per-student alternative.
6. Loan scanners; bundle with scanner OEMs.
7. Publish Bangla OCR benchmarks and workload research for credibility.
8. Track the North Star (verified scripts evaluated) plus agreement and override KPIs.
9. Expand Dhaka → Chattogram → divisional cities → MPO, then WB/Assam → GCC → SEA/Africa.

## Critical assessment

1. **ACV/CAC scale is inconsistent with the other two docs.**
   - GTM assumes ARR of **BDT 750k–1.2M per customer** and CAC of **BDT 45k–210k**.
   - E1 assumes ~**BDT 195–225k** ACV and **BDT 22k** CAC.
   - E3 (ERP) finds mainstream WTP for an *entire ERP* at **BDT 40–65k/yr**, elite EM at **BDT 350k**, with CAC of **BDT 14.5k**.
   - The GTM ACV implies a 2,000-student school spends more on AI grading than elite English-medium schools spend on their whole ERP. **[Analyst note]** It is not credible as a base case; it may hold only for large coaching chains.
2. **Per-student price is implausible for mainstream schools.** BDT 180–300/student/yr is 3–7× the ERP benchmark (Eduman BDT 70–100/student/yr; E3 core tier BDT 42–60/student/yr). It is plausible only for premium EM/EV schools and coaching.
3. **SAM/SOM are constructed, not researched.**
   - The 24 scripts/student/yr is an assumption and contradicts the doc's own ROI example (48).
   - The haircut from $36.4M to $18.4–28.2M has no method.
   - SOM assumes all 280 targets buy at BDT 10/script (100% share).
   - The BANBEIS components do not sum to the stated total.
   - TAM mixes a global AI-in-education figure ($7.05B, which is not assessment) with an unsourced $1.85B regional figure and a boxed "$7.05B–$9.58B".
4. **Missing cost model.** The doc claims 82% software GM and ≥80% GM KPIs without any COGS. HITL labour, scanners (BDT 45k each, loaned free), on-site pilots with parallel manual marking, and CS are not costed. E1's own model shows HITL alone pulls GM to ~59–65%.
5. **Payback arithmetic is inconsistent.** Field sales uses gross profit (150k / (820k/12) = 2.2 mo). ERP partners at 45k/(800k × 0.82/12) would be **0.82 mo**, not 0.7, and inbound at 210k/(750k × 0.82/12) would be **4.1 mo**, not 3.4. Those rows appear to use revenue. Minor, but indicative of unchecked generation.
6. **Internal contradictions.**
   - Pilot size: 500–1,000 (timeline) versus 500–1,500 (Table 8) versus 2,000+ (coaching PoC); E1 says 200.
   - Design partners: "convert 3" (text) versus "2 paid" (Table 13).
   - 10 paid customers in 90 days versus a founder capacity of 2 pilots/month.
   - PLG "not recommended" versus a full PLG free-tier section (25 scripts/mo; E1 says 30).
   - The risk table recommends "flat monthly subscription fees" while the pricing section recommends per-script usage and E3 calls monthly billing unviable.
   - "Hardware loan terms" in negotiation versus "complimentary scanners".
   - Beachhead wording varies: "English-medium" in the exec summary versus "English-version + elite Bangla-medium" in §6.
7. **Beachhead logic has a hole.** **[Analyst note]** The OCR argument (English is easier) favours English-medium/version schools. But coaching chains, the "Very High" adoption-velocity segment, prepare students for SSC/HSC (largely Bangla-medium) and admission tests (largely MCQ/OMR). Udvash-Unmesh's volume may be mostly MCQ, which doesn't need LLM grading. Written practice exists (e.g., HSC creative questions, some engineering admission written tests), but the doc offers no evidence on the written share.
8. **Accuracy claims are misapplied.** "~88% native Bangla handwriting ICR" comes from a vendor site and research papers on isolated characters. An 88% *character* accuracy produces many word errors per answer, and the product sells grading agreement ≥90%. The doc conflates recognition with grading reliability. The competitive table asserts "On-premise / SOC2" with no basis.
9. **ROI savings are soft savings.** BDT 4.2M of "gross salary savings" at BDT 220/h is reclaimed teacher time, not cash. Schools don't reduce salaries. E3 explicitly warns against staff-reduction framing and separates hard and soft savings. The "80% reduction" in turnaround ignores scanning, reviewing and the committee process.
10. **Missing Bangladesh-specific evidence.** There are no interviews with principals or coaching owners, no data on scanner prevalence, no evidence schools will send scripts off-site, no evidence on data-protection or regulatory constraints, no evidence on how internal results are published today, and no verified ERP-vendor partnership interest. The international plan (WB/Assam reuse of the Bangla engine) is reasonable in principle but unsupported.

## Confidence + Relevance to product decisions

- **Confidence: Low–Medium.** Qualitative GTM guidance is sensible and consistent with local norms: relationship selling, owner/MD authority, live demos on the customer's own scripts, time-boxed commitment-backed pilots, ERP co-sell, Oct–Jan budget window, bank/cheque payments. Market sizing, ACV, CAC and funnel rates are largely invented or unsupported, and conflict with E1 and E3.
- **Relevance: High** for product scope:
  - (a) A **live-demo-grade pipeline**: scan 5 scripts and show segmentation plus a rubric score in about 7 minutes. This sets latency targets of roughly **≤1 min/script end-to-end for demo mode**.
  - (b) **Teacher review UI** with confidence flags and mandatory sign-off.
  - (c) **Pilot analytics**: an agreement metric (≥90%), time-saved report and override rate (<10%), built in from day 1.
  - (d) **Feed-scanner and smartphone capture** ingestion, with bulk multi-page segmentation.
  - (e) **ERP/RMS export/API** early (first integration targeted by day 60).
  - (f) English-version-first model readiness, with Bangla following.
  - (g) Data-residency and "no training on student data" commitments.
